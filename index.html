Global Economic Drivers: Trade, Investment, and Growth

Exploratory analysis of global economic trends and interactions between GDP growth and key macroeconomic variables.

1960 2020
How to read the map
  • • Color intensity shows the selected indicator
  • • Darker colors mean higher values
  • • Gray means missing data
  • • Hover for values, click for details

This waterfall chart provides a granular, year-over-year breakdown of how the selected indicator has evolved for a specific country. Unlike a standard line chart, this visualization emphasizes the absolute change between consecutive years, making it easy to identify specific periods of rapid acceleration or significant contraction.

Blue bars represent annual increases, while orange bars indicate a decrease compared to the previous year. The chart "steps" through time, showing how each year's performance contributes to the final total.

Interaction: Hover over any bar to see the specific change and cumulative total for that year. Use your mouse wheel to zoom into specific historical periods for a closer look at volatility.

This chart explores the relationship between a country's internal capital attractiveness and its integration into the global marketplace. By plotting Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) against Trade Openness (both as % of GDP), you can identify distinct economic archetypes, from "Trade Hubs" with high exchange volumes to "Capital Magnets" attracting significant outside investment.

The size of each bubble represents the country's GDP at Purchasing Power Parity (PPP). This adds a layer of "economic weight," allowing you to see if high trade percentages belong to small, specialized nations or large, diversified global powers.

Interaction: Bubbles are clickable, selecting one will update the rest of the dashboard to focus on that country. Zooming allows you to navigate crowded areas of the plot where many countries have similar profiles.

This divergent bar chart offers a side-by-side comparison of a nation's Trade Balance. By mirroring exports and imports across a central axis, it becomes immediately clear whether a country maintains a trade surplus or a trade deficit within its continental context.

Blue bars (Exports) extending to the right represent goods and services sent abroad, while orange bars (Imports) extending to the left represent those brought in. This visualization is essential for understanding regional economic strength and identifying which nations act as the primary "producers" or "consumers" within their continent.

Interaction: All values are scaled to Billions of USD. Hover over any bar for precise figures, or click a country's bar to filter the entire dashboard to that nation's data.
